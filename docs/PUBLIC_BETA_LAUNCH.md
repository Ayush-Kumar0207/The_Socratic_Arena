## Launch description

Most online discussion rewards speed, volume, and certainty. The Socratic Arena is built around a different incentive: make a clear claim, support it, answer the strongest counterargument, and update when the evidence changes.

The public beta combines server-authoritative live debates, AI-assisted multi-judge scoring, evidence and uncertainty signals, targeted practice, rankings, tournaments, campus and classroom workflows, professional simulations, appeals, and portable reasoning credentials. AI decisions remain inspectable and advisory; evidence matching is not presented as proof of truth.

## Maker introduction

We built The Socratic Arena because better thinking needs a place to be practiced, not just discussed. The first version began as a real-time 1v1 debate app. It has grown into Arena OS: one competitive identity across live debates, focused coaching, campus leagues, classrooms, and high-stakes simulations.

This public beta is a validation phase, not a victory lap. We want to learn whether the product helps people make stronger arguments, listen more carefully, and return to practice. Honest feedback—especially about confusing scoring, weak incentives, trust, and onboarding—is welcome.

## Validation scorecard

The next milestone is evidence of use, not additional surface area:

- 100 active public-beta users.
- 1,000 completed debates with quality and abandonment tracked.
- One recurring campus league.
- One institutional classroom or professional pilot.
- A larger calibration set with documented human-versus-panel agreement.
- Weekly review of activation, completed first debate, return rate, appeals, judge disagreement, safety reports, and qualitative feedback.

## Claim guardrails

- Say **AI-assisted judging**, not unbiased or infallible judging.
- Say evidence signals help reviewers inspect claims; do not claim automated truth verification.
- Call the release a **public beta** and invite failure reports.
- Do not publish user emails, private debate content, access tokens, or internal dashboards.
- Use production smoke-test and benchmark results only with their exact scope and date.
- Route vulnerabilities to private GitHub Security Advisories and product feedback to the structured issue forms.
